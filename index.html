Featured Artifact

Knight Capital

August 1, 2012 · $440 Million · 45 Minutes

08:00 AM

Deployment begins

Engineers push new code for a NYSE retail liquidity program. One server is missed.

08:01 AM

Old flag remains

A deprecated “Power Peg” flag is still active on one of eight servers.

09:30 AM

Market opens

The dormant code awakens. Orders begin firing at extreme speed.

09:30 – 09:45

4 million orders

In 45 minutes the system sends millions of orders across 154 stocks.

$0 Accumulated Loss

10:15 AM

Trading halted

Knight Capital’s net capital is effectively wiped out. The firm is sold days later.

What changed

Kill Switches

Mandatory ability to stop all automated trading instantly.

Feature Flags

Strict lifecycle and validation for every configuration flag.

Canary Releases

Never deploy to 100% of capacity at once.

Deployment Validation

Automated checks that every server received the correct code.

Featured Artifact

Silicon Valley Bank

March 2023 · $209 Billion · 48 Hours

2020 – 2021

The deposit surge

Tech and venture clients flood SVB with cash. The bank buys long-duration Treasuries and MBS while rates sit near zero.

2022

Rates rise

The Federal Reserve hikes aggressively. SVB’s bond portfolio suffers massive unrealized losses. Duration risk is no longer theoretical.

March 8, 2023

The reveal

SVB announces a $1.8B realized loss on securities sales and a capital raise. Confidence begins to crack.

March 9

Digital bank run

Social media amplifies fear. $42 billion in deposits try to leave in a single day — the fastest bank run in history.

$0 Deposits Withdrawn (Mar 9)

March 10

Closed by regulators

The FDIC takes over. SVB becomes the second-largest bank failure in U.S. history.

What changed

Interest-Rate Risk

Long-duration “safe” assets can destroy capital when rates move fast.

Uninsured Deposits

Heavy concentration of uninsured funds creates extreme run risk.

Digital Speed

Bank runs now happen in hours, not days. Liquidity buffers must match.

Communication

Capital raises and loss disclosures need careful sequencing and clarity.

  • Never leave dead code paths that can be activated by configuration alone.
  • Every automated trading system requires a hard kill switch reachable in < 1 second.
  • Deployment must be atomic and verified across the entire fleet.
  • Pre-trade risk checks must sit on the critical path, not as optional modules.
  • Canary and progressive rollout are non-negotiable for market-facing systems.
